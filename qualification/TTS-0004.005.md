## Security review

The untrusted boundary is every textual address, trimmed encoding, wildcard,
public-key byte string, and authenticated identity received from outside the
process. No parsed address receives an implicit Domain or Zone. Exact range,
ordering, canonicality, UTF‑8, delimiter, and `p2p` checks run before an
`Address` is constructed. Malformed Unicode wildcard numbers are rejected
without byte-index panics. Collections are reconstructed and compared with
their canonical bytes, preventing alternate signed spellings.

Public keys are fixed-size arrays derived from Libhydrogen's pinned
`hydro_sign_PUBLICKEYBYTES` value and slice conversion rejects every other
length. An anonymous identity is compared by address and key; a non-anonymous
identity is compared by address for TTS‑0004 identity purposes while retaining
its effective key separately for authentication. Anonymous signed-container
grammars require an adjacent key and authenticate before acting on signed data.

Listing is never inferred from anonymous/non-anonymous address shape. Nodelist
membership, an explicit effective pin, and an adapter's trusted Link assessment
are separate inputs. Parsing an accompanying key cannot write the private pin
store. First-contact and continuity pin changes pass through explicit trust
policy, and a continuity update must name the effective predecessor.

Applicable failure paths are covered by the negative tests in the requirement
matrix and by complete branch coverage of the executable TTS‑0004 surface.
TTS‑0004 defines no persistence, concurrent ownership, framing length, or
resource-failure behavior of its own.
