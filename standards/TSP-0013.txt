Abstract
────────

  This document defines the transactional obligations of an adapter
  between native TITH IPC and legacy mailer or tosser storage
  conventions.  It places conversion beyond the IPC boundary in both
  directions and specifies durable ledgers, ownership transfer,
  publication, acknowledgement, replay, and recovery.

1. Introduction
───────────────

  Native TITH does not use a NetMail .MSG directory, Binkley‐style
  outbound, flow file, inbound packet directory, or deletion convention
  as its application interface.  TSP‑0004 IPC is that interface.

  A legacy adapter is an IPC Client.  On the outbound side it consumes
  legacy objects, converts them, and submits native durable work.  On
  the inbound side it claims a native TSP‑0011 item, converts it, and
  durably publishes the objects required by a legacy tosser.

  The adapter may be a separate process or embedded in another
  executable.  Its logical position and ownership rules remain the
  same.  The native mailer does not parse legacy control files or treat
  their pathnames and deletion as native queue state.


2. Adapter Boundary and Ledger
─────────────────────────────

  The adapter maintains a private durable ledger.  It MUST NOT depend
  on a legacy pathname, directory scan order, file timestamp, Subject,
  or disappearance alone as proof of a native IPC result.

  For outbound conversion the ledger records:

    • the race‐resistant identity and digest of every legacy object;
    • the exact conversion policy and resulting canonical submission;
    • TSP‑0003 TITHSIGN and TITHSIG verification and mutable forwarding
      evidence when signed‑region reconstruction succeeds;
    • the ordered normalized Message model, retained Signature, current
      unsigned suffix, selected Local-Identity, and canonical
      submission identity;
    • every Application and Idempotency-Key;
    • each returned JobID and state; and
    • legacy cleanup still required.

  For inbound conversion it records:

    • every InboundID and native PayloadHash;
    • the conversion policy and complete intended legacy object set;
    • the exact signed Message region, Signature, and generated
      TITHSIGN and TITHSIG controls;
    • stable generated legacy names and object digests;
    • every applicable distribution obligation and its Forward Job;
    • staging, publication, and tosser ownership state; and
    • the claim token and acknowledgement recovery state when current.

  Ledger changes are durable before the adapter performs an external
  action which cannot be safely inferred or repeated.  A ledger record
  and legacy objects need not share one filesystem transaction, but the
  recorded state and ordering MUST let recovery distinguish not started,
  staged, published, submitted, acknowledged, and completed work.

  The ledger is private adapter state.  Legacy software and the native
  mailer MUST NOT modify it.  Removing it while work remains permits
  duplication or loss and is an administrative data‐loss operation.


3. Legacy Outbound to Native IPC
──────────────────────────────────

  The adapter opens each selected .MSG, packet, flow file, and attached
  Source exactly once for conversion.  It validates the opened object
  as a stable readable regular file and reads from that same object.
  A pathname replacement MUST NOT select different contents.

  Before changing a legacy object, the adapter durably assigns the
  native submission idempotency keys and records the exact conversion.
  When TSP‑0003 TITHSIG reconstruction succeeds, this record includes
  the unchanged signed Message region, retained Signature, and current
  unsigned legacy controls.  It also records legacy carriage and
  bookkeeping evidence which has no signed mapping.  Only the
  packet‐persistent AttributeWord subset defined by TSP‑0003 maps
  signed LegacyAttributes; changes to all other bits remain recorded
  unsigned evidence.  It then submits the named native fields,
  retained Signature and suffix, selected Local-Identity, and Sources
  through the applicable TSP‑0006 Submit or Submit-Items operation.
  TITHSIGN and TITHSIG are not duplicated as generic controls.  The
  mailer receives no request to interpret a .MSG, packet, tic, or flow
  file.

  The adapter MUST NOT remove, truncate, mark sent, or otherwise retire
  a legacy object until a complete Committed result or
  Lookup-Submission proves that the corresponding native Job exists.
  An incomplete IPC transaction leaves ownership uncertain and is
  recovered only through the same idempotency keys.

  A legacy Source disposition may be mapped to TSP‑0006 Delete or
  Truncate only when its semantics are exact, the capability is
  advertised, and the adapter intends cleanup after confirmed remote
  delivery.  Otherwise the adapter uses Copy with Keep and records its
  own later legacy cleanup.  It MUST NOT silently weaken a requested
  ownership or deletion rule.  FLAGS KFS and TFS are evidence of these
  legacy sender‐side rules; they do not by themselves grant native
  deletion or truncation authority.  K/S applies to the containing
  message rather than its attached Sources and is tracked separately.

  Retiring a .MSG or control entry and recording its associated JobID
  are one recoverable adapter operation.  After a crash, the adapter
  uses JobID, Lookup-Submission, and its ledger rather than submitting a
  newly generated Message from the same legacy object.


4. Native Inbound to a Legacy Tosser
────────────────────────────────────

  The adapter obtains an item with TSP‑0012 Claim-Inbound and verifies
  the complete payload size and hash.  It checks its ledger for
  InboundID before converting or publishing anything.

  Before publication it applies the configured TSP‑0011 final item
  authentication policy.  Deliver-Warn records the exact diagnostic in
  the ledger and places it at the start of the locally delivered legacy
  Message presentation.  The adapter retains the unchanged native
  payload separately.  The diagnostic is not an input to TITHSIG
  reconstruction and MUST NOT be included if the object is later used
  to reconstruct or forward the original native item.  For a File, the
  diagnostic is an adjacent local message or metadata; it MUST NOT
  alter the companion file bytes.

  Orphan publishes no addressed legacy object.  The adapter first
  records the exact payload and reason in its private administrative
  store, then acknowledges the claim.  Reply-Origin is committed as a
  separate TSP‑0006 Job before acknowledgement and obeys the loop and
  flood controls in TSP‑0011.

  If the ledger shows that the exact InboundID and PayloadHash were
  already published and durably transferred to adapter ownership, the
  adapter does not publish a duplicate.  It acknowledges the current
  claim and completes any interrupted ledger update.

  For a new InboundID, the adapter durably records the intended
  conversion and generated legacy names before publication.  It
  converts the exact claimed TITH item according to TSP‑0003 and trusted
  local policy.  For a Message, the intended object set includes the
  TITHSIGN and TITHSIG reconstruction controls required to preserve its
  byte‐exact signed region and Signature.  Remote Filename, Subject, Area,
  MessageID, and pathlike strings MUST NOT become local path components
  without the explicit validation and name generation required by the
  legacy format.

  If that conversion publishes attached Sources, the ledger records
  whether legacy processing is to keep, remove, or truncate each source.
  The adapter emits FLAGS KFS or TFS only for the corresponding legacy
  post‐send action and never emits both.  Those controls do not alter the
  native Message or File and are not inputs to TITHSIG signed‐region
  reconstruction.

  The adapter acknowledges the inbound item only after all converted
  objects and the ledger state which transfers responsibility from the
  mailer are durable.  Its retained source MUST be private from the
  legacy tosser; a published object which the tosser may remove is not
  by itself recoverable adapter state.  At acknowledgement the adapter
  owns delivery to the legacy tosser even if that tosser has not
  processed the objects yet.  It MUST recover and re‐present them
  without depending on the mailer's retained payload.

  For an Origin-Valid or SignedOrigin-Valid EchoMail or file
  distribution item, the ledger also records its applicable
  distribution obligation.  Before acknowledging the item, the adapter
  MUST commit the native copies with TSP‑0006 Job Forward while the
  claim remains current and record the resulting Job.  The Forward Job
  preserves the exact signed bytes and Signature.  The published legacy
  object is terminal local delivery; the adapter and legacy tosser MUST
  NOT use it to create onward copies.

  An Unsigned, Origin-Invalid, or SignedOrigin-Invalid item has no
  onward distribution obligation.  Its Deliver-Warn or Orphan outcome
  is final-delivery or administrative work, and neither the adapter nor
  the legacy tosser creates an onward copy from it.

  An adapter MAY retain the native claim until the legacy tosser
  completes terminal local delivery, but it must renew the claim,
  preserve InboundID idempotency, and prevent another worker from
  publishing the same objects.  Legacy file disappearance by itself is
  not a sufficient durable acknowledgement record.

  A temporary conversion or publication failure uses Defer-Inbound or
  Release-Inbound.  A permanent conversion refusal uses Reject-Inbound
  only when trusted policy authorizes the local terminal outcome.
  Neither result changes the already completed TITH transport Accepted.


5. Legacy Publication and Batching
──────────────────────────────────

  Every legacy object is first constructed under a private temporary
  name on the destination filesystem.  Its contents and metadata are
  made durable before it is atomically published under an unused final
  name without replacing an existing object.  The containing directory
  update is then made durable.

  When a packet or control object refers to companion files, every
  companion is durably published first and the referencing object is
  published last.  A legacy tosser MUST NOT be able to observe a packet
  whose required companion is still temporary or incomplete.

  Generated names are stable ledger data derived from locally generated
  identity, not an arbitrary remote pathname.  A name collision causes
  another unused name to be selected and recorded before publication.
  Recovery never overwrites an unrelated existing object.

  An adapter may place several InboundIDs in one legacy packet.  Its
  ledger records the exact ordered set and conversion digest before the
  packet is published.  It acknowledges each native item only after the
  shared packet and ledger are durable.  A crash between
  acknowledgements may redeliver an unacknowledged InboundID; the ledger
  recognizes it as already present and prevents a second packet entry.

  Packet aggregation is legacy presentation only.  It does not merge
  InboundIDs, native payloads, claims, authentication results, or local
  outcomes.

  The adapter does not retire its private packet source merely because
  the published packet disappeared.  It marks the legacy toss complete
  only from the durable completion indication defined by its configured
  legacy interface or from direct verification that every required
  output is durable.


6. Failure and Recovery
───────────────────────

  On startup the adapter recovers its ledger before scanning for new
  legacy work or claiming another inbound item.  It validates every
  retained object by its recorded race‐resistant identity, size, and
  digest rather than assuming that a pathname still identifies it.

  An outbound entry without a known Submit result is resolved with
  Lookup-Submission.  Existing completes the Job association.  NotFound
  permits resubmission only while every required Source and ownership
  condition still holds.

  An inbound entry with staged but unpublished objects is completed or
  rolled back according to its ledger.  An entry with published objects
  is never published again.  If Acknowledge-Inbound is uncertain, the
  adapter repeats it with the same token or uses Query-Inbound.

  If the legacy tosser altered, removed, or replaced a published object,
  the adapter follows its configured legacy ownership rule.  It MUST
  NOT request the mailer to recreate an item which it already
  acknowledged merely because legacy processing lost the adapter's
  copy.

  A malformed legacy input or unsupported conversion is retained for
  diagnosis or explicitly rejected according to policy.  The adapter
  MUST NOT guess missing addresses, silently discard attachment data,
  or claim successful conversion to make a queue advance.


7. Conversion Scope and Limitations
────────────────────────────────────

  TSP‑0003 owns the byte‐level mapping between native TITH items and
  legacy FTN formats.  This document owns only the placement,
  transaction, ownership, and recovery boundary around that mapping.
  A deployed legacy dialect does not change a native IPC grammar.

  TSP‑0003 revision 2 defines the canonical stored Message, packed
  Message, Type‑2+ packet, WaZOO request, and TIC conversion, including
  the trusted context which supplies facts absent from legacy bytes.
  Its TITHSIGN and TITHSIG controls permit a canonical Message
  conversion to restore the exact original encoded children covered by
  Signature and the original Signature itself.  Children following
  Signature are rebuilt from current legacy state.  The adapter ledger
  retains unmapped
  carriage and bookkeeping changes separately; it MUST NOT apply them
  to signed values.  This lossless rule applies to TITH‑to‑legacy‑to‑TITH
  conversion.  Ordinary Legacy‑to‑TITH‑to‑legacy conversion need not
  reproduce the source legacy bytes.
  Local policy selects objects, links, names, character defaults, and
  accepted input dialects only where TSP‑0003 expressly permits it.  It
  MUST NOT be presented as another native TITH wire grammar.

  The TSP‑0006 retained Message Job is the transaction boundary for
  that reconstruction.  The adapter records the canonical Job before
  submission, including the exact Signature and Local-Identity.  The
  Service independently reconstructs and verifies the signed bytes,
  replaces only the unsigned RequestIdentifier, retains current
  unsigned controls, and appends the selected local Via.  A different
  Idempotency-Key does not authorize a second delivery of the same
  authenticated signed-item identity.

  At the legacy FTN boundary, exported EchoMail and file distribution
  network (FDN) transfers are NetMail traffic.  They use individual
  NetMail messages, packets, file attaches, and TIC conventions
  described by FTS‑0004.001 version 3.31, FTS‑5006.001 revision 1, and
  FSC‑0028.001.
  The adapter classifies what that legacy NetMail carriage represents.
  Ordinary NetMail uses TSP‑0006 Submit or a Submit-Items NetMail Job;
  EchoMail and FDN use the corresponding Submit-Items Kind.  A file
  transfer belonging to no area and to no message uses the Peer-File
  Kind, and a converted WaZOO request uses the FileRequest Kind, one
  Job per action.  This preserves the legacy carriage fact without
  applying TITH NetMail Destination routing to an item whose native
  semantics require explicit area links, and without inventing an area
  for a transfer which has none.

  Compatibility tolerance for malformed packets, alternate headers,
  character sets, control paragraphs, .MSG variants, and flow file
  dialects remains in the adapter and TSP‑0003.  It MUST NOT weaken
  validation of TSP‑0004 IPC or TSP‑0011 native payloads.


8. Security
────────

  Legacy directories and control files are untrusted input even when
  they are local.  Every lookup, open, publication, replacement check,
  and removal uses race‐resistant host facilities.  Symbolic links,
  devices, directories, pipes, and sockets are rejected where a regular
  file is required.

  The adapter authorizes native operations through its configured IPC
  principal and Application.  Its configured SignedOrigin is the local
  effective signer when the legacy Origin has no key.  Legacy origin
  fields, usernames, packet passwords, flow commands, and filenames do
  not grant native routing, Source, cleanup, or Application authority.

  The adapter ledger, temporary objects, generated names, claimed
  payloads, and conversion diagnostics are protected from other local
  principals.  Local Source and export pathnames SHOULD be omitted from
  ordinary logs.


9. Feedback and Discussion
───────────────────────────

  Keeping the adapter on the Client side makes the ownership boundary
  symmetrical.  Legacy applications see the conventions they already
  implement, while the mailer sees only authenticated IPC transactions
  and durable native items.

  The ledger is necessary because legacy file publication and deletion
  do not provide an atomic transaction with the native queue.  Stable
  JobID, InboundID, and idempotency keys let the adapter bridge that gap
  without making either side infer state from the other's spool.
