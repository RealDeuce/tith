Abstract

  Current practice for Fidonet Technology Networks (FTN) is to
  maintain a nodelist used to store the details of the nodes in the
  network, and the network structure.

1. Introduction
───────────────

  Fidonet is a peer‐to‐peer network utilising multiple different, and
  often incompatible, communication technologies that are independent
  of Fidonet itself, and is organised into a particular hierarchy for
  both technical and management purposes.

  For direct communication, it is necessary that the originating node
  knows in advance the exact method and capabilities of the
  destination node in order to determine if, when, and how a direct
  mail session can be established.  For routing and management, the
  stated hierarchy must also be known.

  All this information is collected into a file called the "nodelist".

  While nodelists of various flavours and completeness are in use, the
  "Distribution Nodelist" is the official record of all Fidonet nodes,
  their operators, their capabilities and their relation to each other
  in the network hierarchy.  In essence, the nodelist defines Fidonet.

3. Purpose
──────────

  Along with the companion technical standard TTS‑5001, this
  document defines the format and content of Fidonet's distribution
  nodelist.

  Unlike most TITH documents, the nodelist standards are not only
  aimed at developers, but also at maintainers of Fidonet (and other
  Fidonet Technology Networks) nodelist segments.  Both producers and
  consumers MUST use the exact grammar in this document.  A consumer
  MUST reject a data line which does not conform; compatibility with
  an FTS‑5000.005 source belongs at an explicit conversion boundary.

  Except where this document specifies a limit, a field or line has no
  fixed maximum length.  Platform overflow checks, allocation failure,
  and local resource policy remain valid reasons an implementation may
  be unable to process a nodelist safely; such a failure does not make
  otherwise conforming data malformed.

  It is assumed you're already familiar with Fidonet structure and
  addressing terminology.


4. Publication and Distribution
───────────────────────────────

  The Distribution Nodelist uses the filename DDD-nodelist.nnn, where
  DDD is the domain text and nnn is exactly three ASCII decimal digits
  giving the Gregorian ordinal day of publication in the range 001–366.
  U+002D HYPHEN-MINUS and U+002E FULL STOP are literal filename syntax.
  Partial or alternate nodelists MUST use another base filename in
  place of DDD-nodelist.

  Distribution compresses the exact DDD-nodelist.nnn bytes as one
  ordinary Zstandard frame conforming to RFC 8878.  The resulting file
  is named DDD-nodelist.nnn.zst.  It is not an archive containing an
  intermediate file format.

  The frame MUST decode without a dictionary.  It MUST NOT be a
  skippable frame and MUST NOT be followed by another frame or trailing
  data.  Compression parameters may vary, and the RFC 8878 content
  checksum may be present or absent.  Other representations may be
  distributed, but they are not TTS‑5000 encodings.

  Every node on a network SHOULD make its current nodelist available
  by FileRequest under the name DDD-nodelist.zst.


5. Content
──────────

  The nodelist is a flat text file containing any number of lines,
  encoded in UTF‑8.

  U+0000 through U+001F and U+007F through U+009F are prohibited.
  U+0009 CHARACTER TABULATION is permitted only as the field separator
  on a data line, and U+000A LINE FEED is permitted only as a line
  terminator.  Every line MUST have that terminator.  Every other
  Unicode scalar value is permitted unless a field grammar below
  restricts it further.


5.1. Comment Lines
──────────────────

  A comment line begins with U+003B SEMICOLON.  Its interest‐flag
  prefix is the maximal consecutive run of ASCII letters U+0041
  through U+005A and U+0061 through U+007A immediately following the
  semicolon.  A program which processes the nodelist may use those
  flags to determine the disposition of the comment.  The remainder
  is uninterpreted free‐form text.

  Five uppercase interest flags have assigned meanings.  Every other
  ASCII letter remains valid but has no meaning assigned here.

     ;S This comment is of particular interest to Sysops.

     ;U This comment is of particular interest to BBS users.

     ;F This comment should appear in any formatted "Fido List."

     ;A This comment is of general interest (shorthand for ;SUF).

     ;E This comment is an error message inserted by a nodelist
        generating program.

     ;  This comment may be ignored by a nodelist processor.

  A nodelist has no header line.  The first line of a file is an
  ordinary line of whichever kind it is, and no line carries a
  meaning the same line would not carry elsewhere in the same file.

  No value in the free‐form remainder is interpreted.  A processor
  MUST NOT validate that text or refuse a nodelist because it resembles
  metadata which does not match the file.


5.2.  Data lines
────────────────

  A nodelist data line contains exactly eleven (11) variable length
  fields separated by tabs (U+0009), defined below.  Each data line is
  a record for an individual Fidonet node.

  All scalar values permitted by section 5 are allowed in string fields
  unless specified otherwise.

  Field 1: Keyword

    Type: string.

    The keyword field defines the type of node, and will either be
    empty or contain exactly one of the keywords defined below,
    grouped by functional class.

    No other keyword is valid in a Distribution Nodelist or TTS‑5000
    segment.  A separately specified private format may reuse the
    record shape and define other keywords, but that format and its
    profile selection are outside TTS‑5000.  A TTS‑5000 consumer MUST
    reject an unassigned keyword.

    Full or Member Nodes — these are the system numbers of individual
    Fidonet members.

      <empty>
        Defines an operational member having at least one usable
        direct‐contact target.

      Pvt
        Defines an operational member having no usable direct‐contact
        target.  A member represented by either the empty or Pvt
        alternative MUST use Pvt if and only if it has no such target;
        otherwise its keyword MUST be empty.

        A usable direct‐contact target is any of:

          • a nonempty telephone number in field 6;
          • an Internet Protocol or IIH occurrence which, after the
            TTS‑5001 resolution rules are applied, has both a server
            and a usable port; or
          • an Email Flag which carries an address directly or through
            the addressed IEM rules in TTS‑5001.

        INA alone, a port without a server, a server for a method having
        no default or explicit port, a bare Email method, PSTN or ISDN
        capability flags without a telephone number, and
        `IIH:<PublicKey>` without an endpoint are not usable targets.
        Pvt entries are permitted only as members of local networks.

      Hold
        Defines a node which is operational but cannot be publicly
        contacted.  This is expected to be a temporary condition.

      Down
        Defines a node which is not operational.  This is expected to
        be permanent or semipermanent.

    Routing consequences of these states are outside this document.

    Administrative Nodes — these nodes indicate the beginning of a
    branch in the Fidonet addressing and management hierarchy.

    Each branch may contain one or more administrative nodes of lower
    tier, or any of the above member node types unless stated
    otherwise.

    Since the nodelist is a flat file, a branch is simply terminated
    at the next administrative node of equal or higher tier (or EOF).

    Member nodes are always part of an administrative branch, and may
    never appear at the top level of the nodelist.

    In order from highest to lowest:

      Zone
        Begins the definition of a geographic zone and defines its
        coordinator.  A zone is also a logical region AND net.

        Normal nodes immediately below a Zone node, and before any
        other administrative nodes, are Zone Independent Nodes.

      Region
        Begins the definition of a geographic region and defines its
        coordinator.  A region is also a logical net.

        Normal nodes immediately below a Region node, and before any
        other administrative nodes, are Region Independent Nodes.

      Host
        Begins the definition of a local network (net) and normally
        defines its coordinator unless section 5.3 assigns that role
        to another entry.

      Hub
        Begins the definition of a routing subunit within a multilevel
        local network.

    A nonempty Distribution Nodelist begins, after any comments, with a
    Zone data line.  Before that line there is no active zone or net.
    Zone supplies both its zone and logical‐net context.  Region
    requires an active Zone and supplies its region and logical‐net
    context.  Host requires an active Zone, may occur directly within
    a Zone or Region branch, and starts a local net.  Hub requires an
    active Host and starts a subunit of that Host's local net; it MUST
    NOT occur directly within a Zone or Region branch.

    Members immediately below Zone and Region are Zone Independent and
    Region Independent Nodes respectively.  Members below Host or Hub
    are members of that local net.  An administrative entry ends every
    preceding branch of equal or lower tier.  Region and Host numbers
    share one logical‐net address namespace within a zone.


  Field 2: Node number

     Type: integer;  Range: 1 to 32767.

     The encoded value is one ASCII digit from U+0031 through U+0039
     followed by zero or more ASCII digits from U+0030 through U+0039.
     It contains no sign, whitespace, leading zero, or other code point.
     The range therefore makes every valid encoding one through five
     bytes without a separate length restriction.

     For member nodes and Hubs (which are treated as member nodes as
     far as addressing goes), this number is the node number, and must
     be unique within their local network.

     For a Zone entry, this number is the zone number.  A region and
     net number of equal value is implied, and the node number is
     zero.  Zone numbers must be unique across the entire network.

     For a Region entry, this number is the region number.  A net
     number of equal value is implied, and the node number is zero.
     Region numbers must be unique within each zone.

     For a Host entry, this number is the net number, and the node
     number is zero.  As with Region numbers, net numbers must be
     unique within each zone.

     Because the node number zero is reserved for Zone, Region and
     Host entries, the value "0" is strictly forbidden in this field.


  Field 3: Node name

     Type: string.

     This is the name by which the system is known.


  Field 4: Location

     Type: string.

     This is the physical location of the node.

     Generally, this is expressed as the primary local area (town,
     suburb, city, etc.) and where applicable, a comma and space,
     followed by the abbreviation of the regional geopolitical
     administrative district (state, province, department, county,
     etc.).


  Field 5: Sysop name

     Type: string.

     This is the name of the Fidonet member responsible for the node.

     The name SHOULD be a valid user of the node.  That is to say that
     netmail addressed to this name should reach a Sysop.


  Field 6: Phone number (PSTN or ISDN)

     Type: string;  Encoded length: zero or 3–29 bytes;  Characters:
     ASCII digits and U+002D HYPHEN-MINUS.

     This field is either empty, or contains two or more nonempty
     subfields of ASCII digits separated by one U+002D HYPHEN-MINUS.
     U+002D is literal syntax.  The first field is the country code.
     Conventionally, the remaining components follow local practice.

     The various parts of the phone number may be used to derive cost
     and routing information, as well as what number is to be dialled.
     A typical example of the data in a phone number field is
     1-800-555-0100, corresponding to country 1 (USA), area 800 (area
     code), exchange 555, and number 0100.

     An empty field supplies no PSTN or ISDN direct-contact target.


  Field 7: System Flags

     Type: string.

     Contains exactly the System Flags defined by TTS‑5001, separated
     and ordered as that document specifies.

  Field 8: PSTN and ISDN Flags

     Type: string.

     Contains exactly the PSTN and ISDN Flags defined by TTS‑5001,
     separated and ordered as that document specifies.

  Field 9: Internet Flags

     Type: string.

     Contains exactly the Internet Flags defined by TTS‑5001,
     separated and ordered as that document specifies.  TTS‑5001 also
     defines how INA supplies omitted servers and how repeated
     endpoints retain preference order.

     The PublicKey in an IIH flag is the key used to authenticate TITH
     data whose Origin is this entry's address.  An item's SignedOrigin
     cannot override this key; it is considered only when no Origin key
     is available.

     `IIH:<PublicKey>` publishes the key but no endpoint and therefore
     remains valid on a Pvt entry; see field 1.

     When a using context has accepted this nodelist as authoritative
     for the address, the IIH PublicKey supplies the authoritative
     external anchor defined by TTS‑0008.  That document determines
     whether the anchor or an authenticated successor is the effective
     key.  TTS‑5000 does not define how a nodelist publication is
     authenticated or accepted.

     When a node is first published following an application from the
     anonymous address defined in TTS‑0004, this PublicKey MUST be
     the same PublicKey that accompanied the Origin of the application
     Message.

  Field 10: Email Flags

     Type: string.

     Contains exactly the Email Flags defined by TTS‑5001, separated
     and ordered as that document specifies.

  Field 11: Other Flags

     Type: string.

     Contains exactly the Other Flags and extensions defined by
     TTS‑5001, separated and ordered as that document specifies.


5.3. Flag Relationships
───────────────────────

  On one data line, at most one of XA, XB, XC, XP, XR, XW, and XX may
  occur.  CM MUST NOT occur with ICM, a Mail Period flag, or an Online
  Period flag.  These constraints avoid contradictory statements; an
  implementation MUST NOT infer or remove a redundant capability.

  ZEC may occur on at most one entry in a Zone branch and identifies
  that Zone's EchoMail Coordinator.  REC and RPK may each occur on at
  most one entry in a Region branch and identify that Region's
  EchoMail Coordinator and Pointlist Keeper respectively.

  NEC and NPK may each occur on at most one entry in a logical net and
  identify that net's EchoMail Coordinator and Pointlist Keeper
  respectively.  NC may occur on at most one nonadministrative member
  entry in a local net.  When present, it identifies that net's Network
  Coordinator in place of the Host entry.  It MUST NOT occur on a Host,
  Hub, Zone Independent Node, or Region Independent Node.


6. Segments
───────────

  A segment is a partial nodelist containing one or more complete
  branches.  Its first data line is the relevant Zone, Region, Host, or
  Hub entry.  It uses the same record grammar and hierarchy transitions
  as the Distribution Nodelist.

  Parsing or producing a segment begins with an externally supplied
  domain and, according to its first entry, these additional values:

    • a Zone segment needs no initial zone or net;
    • a Region or Host segment needs an initial zone; and
    • a Hub segment needs an initial zone and local net.

  Those initial values provide the omitted enclosing hierarchy only.
  TTS‑5000 does not define how they are discovered or authenticated.

  The format and structure is the same as the Distribution Nodelist,
  with a base filename of local invention, usually derived from the
  contents of the segment (e.g. fidonet-N712list.nnn or
  fidonet-N712seg.nnn for a segment containing all the nodes in Net
  712 of fidonet).

  Segment transport, authentication, merge policy, conflict handling,
  and administrative workflow are outside this document.  A compiled
  Distribution Nodelist MUST independently satisfy every complete‐file
  rule in sections 4 and 5.

   defined contextual flag combinations and coordinator cardinality.

   external anchor supplied to TTS‑0008 effective key resolution.

   Rev.9, 2026‑08‑23: Defined exact publication framing, character and
