Abstract
────────

  This document defines the operational obligations asserted by the
  TTS‑5001 ITX and ISE nodelist flags.  It requires their receipt
  facilities to be enabled, sets the maximum receipt delay, and defines
  the relationship between an ISE occurrence and its advertised
  encodings.  It does not redefine either email tunnelling method or
  put service operation back into the flag‑list grammar.

1. Introduction
───────────────

  TTS‑5001 defines a flag as a canonical assertion and deliberately
  leaves operation of the facility to another standard.  Its ITX and
  ISE flags retain the FTS‑5001.006 meanings “TransX encoding with
  return receipts” and “SEAT tunnelling with return receipts”.  The
  older registry also required those receipts to be enabled and sent
  within 24 hours, and recommended accompanying ISE with an advertised
  transfer encoding.

  Those are service requirements, not properties of a comma‑separated
  flag list.  This proposal owns them without changing the spelling,
  order, address resolution, or interpretation of any TTS‑5001 flag.


2. Applicability
────────────────

  This document applies to every email endpoint at which a system
  advertises ITX or ISE through a TTS‑5001 flag occurrence.  TTS‑5001
  resolves an occurrence with no explicit address through addressed
  IEM occurrences.  The obligations here apply independently at every
  resulting effective address and at an explicitly addressed
  occurrence.

  A bare occurrence for which TTS‑5001 supplies no effective address
  remains a capability assertion but supplies no reachable endpoint.
  It does not make some unadvertised email address the default.

  This document does not define SMTP, Internet mail addressing,
  TransX encoding, or SEAT encoding.  It constrains the receipt
  behavior of a system which has already selected and implemented the
  advertised method.


3. Common Receipt Obligation
────────────────────────────

  A system advertising ITX or ISE MUST enable that method's positive
  receipt facility at every applicable endpoint.  It MUST
  automatically submit the method‑defined positive receipt no later
  than 24 elapsed hours after it has received a complete valid file.

  A complete valid file is one for which every part required by the
  method has arrived, the file has been reconstructed, and every
  integrity check required by that method has succeeded.  Merely
  receiving an email message, one part of a multipart file, or bytes
  which fail a required integrity check does not start the 24‑hour
  period.

  Submission means handing the receipt to the configured outbound
  email service for the sender address identified by the method.  This
  proposal does not claim that Internet email delivers it within that
  period.  A receipt acknowledges the identified file or file part; an
  SMTP delivery notification or human reply which cannot be correlated
  by the sending implementation is not that receipt.

  A negative receipt, retransmission, duplicate, cancellation, or
  malformed transfer remains subject to the selected method.  None is
  a substitute for the required positive receipt after successful
  receipt.


4. TransX
───────────

  ITX selects TransX encoding and its acknowledgment facility.  A
  positive receipt under section 3 is the TransX acknowledgment which
  causes the paired sending implementation to record successful
  delivery of the identified file.

  FSP‑1012.003 names TransX as an email method with optional
  acknowledgment messages but does not assign one interoperable
  transfer or acknowledgment grammar.  This proposal does not invent a
  second TransX representation.  Conformance is therefore assessed
  using the paired TransX profile selected by the endpoints; that
  profile does not become an implicit TITH standard.


5. SEAT
────────

  ISE selects the SEAT protocol in FTS‑1025.001 with its level‑two
  receipt facility enabled.  For each successfully decoded segment,
  the positive receipt is:

    Ftn-Receipt: <Ftn-Seg-Id> Ack

  The field names, identifier, case handling, grouping of receipts,
  and email representation are exactly those of FTS‑1025.001.  Every
  segment of a complete valid file MUST have its positive receipt
  submitted by the section 3 deadline.  A receipt MAY be submitted as
  soon as its segment has been validated.

  For every effective email address advertised by ISE, the same flag
  list SHOULD contain an IUC or IMI occurrence which TTS‑5001 resolves
  to that address.  It MAY contain both.  A method flag which resolves
  only to another address does not satisfy this recommendation.


6. Scope of the Claim
─────────────────────

  ITX or ISE asserts the section 3 behavior for as long as the flag is
  published.  A system whose corresponding receiver or automatic
  positive‑receipt path is disabled does not satisfy that assertion.

  IUC and IMI by themselves do not assert a receipt service.  IEM,
  EVY, EMA, and the Internet protocol flags likewise gain no receipt
  requirement from this document.  This proposal defines no receipt
  obligation for a temporary transport failure or a transfer which
  never became a complete valid file.


7. Feedback and Discussion
───────────────────────────

  The nodelist tells a sender which method and address a system claims.
  This proposal tells the claimant what it must do after successful
  receipt.  Keeping the two concerns separate lets TTS‑5001 remain a
  stable registry and permits a receipt implementation to be tested
  without treating flag parsing as service operation.

  TransX remains less completely specified than SEAT.  That is an
  interoperability limitation of the named legacy method, not
  permission for TTS‑5001 to define an implementation by implication.
