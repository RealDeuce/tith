Abstract
────────

  This document defines durable inbound delivery items owned by a TITH
  mailer.  It specifies their identity, exact native payload, local
  consumer commitment, states, exclusive claims, application outcomes,
  duplicate handling, recovery, and retention.

1. Introduction
───────────────

  An inbound delivery item is the mailer's durable unit of work for a
  local Message, standalone File, or FileRequest which has passed the
  TITH transport boundary.  It is the native replacement for placing a
  packet in a tosser's inbound directory.

  The item remains owned by the mailer until a configured local
  consumer durably accepts responsibility for it.  Nonnormatively,
  TSP‑0012 provides local IPC operations which claim and resolve items,
  and TSP‑0013 describes a legacy adapter beyond that IPC boundary.

  An inbound item is not a Bundle, packet, or temporary connection
  object.  Bundle framing and immediate transport routing are complete
  before local consumption.  A consumer processes the exact native
  item and creates any new outbound work through the applicable
  submission operation rather than modifying the inbound item.


2. Inbound Identity and Data
────────────────────────────

  Every inbound item has one stable InboundID.  An InboundID is the
  uppercase ASCII letter "I" followed by exactly 32 lowercase
  hexadecimal digits representing a randomly generated 128‑bit value.
  A collision with any retained InboundID causes another value to be
  generated.

  InboundID remains unchanged across claims, release, deferral,
  restart, payload export, adapter conversion, and terminal state.  It
  is local metadata and MUST NOT be inserted into the native item or
  transmitted to a remote TITH node.

  One inbound item owns one exact encoded TLV of type Message, File, or
  FileRequest as defined by TTS‑0009.  Payload begins with that item's
  canonical Type and ends with the final byte of its Value.  It does
  not include the enclosing SignedTLV, Bundle Header, another sibling
  item, or connection framing.  Those exact payload bytes are immutable
  for the life of the item.

  The inbound record also contains:

    • the configured local consumer Application;
    • the receiving local identity;
    • the authenticated immediate peer address and public key;
    • the time at which the receiver committed the item;
    • Message, File, or FileRequest as its Kind;
    • the end‐to‐end item authentication result when applicable;
    • the payload byte count and hash; and
    • its state, claim history, outstanding local delivery, routing,
      and distribution responsibilities, and most recent local result.

  The receiving local identity is the exact local identity represented
  by the Destination in the Bundle Header.  The immediate peer is the
  authenticated top‐level Origin of the Bundle which carried the item.
  Its recorded key is the exact key used to authenticate that peer:
  the accompanying key for an anonymous identity or the effective key
  resolved under TTS‑0008 for a non‐anonymous identity.  It does not
  replace the Origin or PublicKey inside a Message or standalone File.

  ItemAuthentication is one of these values for a Message or
  standalone File:

    Unsigned
    SignedOrigin-Invalid
    SignedOrigin-Valid
    Origin-Invalid
    Origin-Valid

  Unsigned means the item has no end‐to‐end Signature.  Origin-Valid and
  Origin-Invalid mean Origin was the effective signer and Signature
  respectively did or did not verify over the exact received signed
  bytes under its effective key.  SignedOrigin-Valid and
  SignedOrigin-Invalid have the same meanings when SignedOrigin was the
  effective signer.  A verification failure establishes no cause: it
  does not establish that the Signature was ever valid, that another
  byte sequence was authenticated, or that the item or Signature was
  modified in transit.  The value is Transport for a FileRequest, which
  has no independent item Signature by design.

  Origin-Valid and SignedOrigin-Valid meet the authenticated relay and
  distribution predicate defined by TSP‑0002.  Unsigned,
  Origin-Invalid, and SignedOrigin-Invalid do not.  These storage names
  record the TTS‑0009 authentication result; they do not define that
  routing predicate.

  None of these values reports Bundle authentication.  Before an item
  reaches this record, its enclosing payload SignedTLV has been
  authenticated with the Bundle Origin.  Unauthenticated SignedData is
  never made an inbound item.  The immediate peer therefore attests to
  the exact bytes it sent even when the enclosed item is Unsigned or
  has an invalid end‐to‐end Signature.

  PayloadHash is the 32‑byte unkeyed libhydrogen generic hash of the
  exact payload bytes.  Its context is the eight bytes of the C string
  "InItem1", including its trailing NUL, and its key is NULL.


3. Transport Acceptance and Consumer Commitment
───────────────────────────────────────────────────

  This document applies when trusted local configuration selects local
  application delivery for a Message, standalone File, or FileRequest
  from an authenticated payload SignedTLV.  A value committed for
  forwarding or holding to a next hop remains routing work and is not
  an inbound item.

  Before returning TTS‑0005 Accepted for a new locally delivered value,
  the receiver MUST atomically and durably store:

    • the complete immutable payload;
    • InboundID and every inbound record field;
    • the selected consumer Application;
    • Available as the initial state;
    • the TTS‑0009 signed‑item identity when the item has one; and
    • every outstanding local delivery, routing, or distribution
      responsibility selected for the item.

  A consumer Application is selected from trusted local configuration,
  not from an unauthenticated field or IPC request.  Configuration may
  use native item Kind, local Destination, user, Area, authenticated
  identity, ItemAuthentication, or other validated data.  If local
  delivery is selected but no consumer or administrative quarantine can
  own the item durably, the receiver MUST reject it rather than accept
  and discard it.

  Transport Accepted means the receiver owns durable responsibility
  for the item.  It does not mean that a tosser imported it, a user read
  it, an Area accepted it, or a legacy packet was created.  A later
  local outcome MUST NOT retract, replace, or contradict the completed
  transport response.

  Responsibility for the exact item may move to another durable owner
  only through an atomic transfer which satisfies TSP‑0002 section 9.
  The inbound record remains responsible for every obligation which
  that transfer has not discharged or assigned to another durable
  owner.

  A transport duplicate handled under TTS‑0005 section 7 does not create
  another inbound item.  The receiver returns Accepted for the current
  transmission without importing, forwarding, or delivering the
  duplicate again.

  Poll values are handled by the mailer and are not inbound items.
  Accepted and Rejected response values are transport results and are
  likewise not inbound items.  Values returned by an accepted Poll or
  FileRequest become inbound items only when their own type and local
  disposition satisfy this section.


4. States and Claims
─────────────────────

  An inbound item has exactly one externally visible state:

    Available
    Claimed
    Deferred
    Consumed
    Rejected
    Failed

  Available is durable and eligible for a consumer claim.  Deferred is
  unclaimed and becomes eligible at its recorded retry Timestamp.
  Claimed is durably and exclusively owned by one consumer worker until
  its claim expires or is resolved.

  A claim records the authenticated caller, Application, ClaimKey,
  ClaimToken, expiration Timestamp, and claim attempt number.  ClaimKey
  is a nonempty arbitrary‐length UTF‑8 string supplied by the consumer.
  ClaimToken is the uppercase ASCII letter "C" followed by exactly 32
  lowercase hexadecimal digits representing a random 128‑bit value.

  The Application and ClaimKey form a durable idempotency identity.
  Once that identity selects an item, repeating it MUST NOT select
  another item.  Its mapping to InboundID, ClaimToken, and outcome is
  retained until the inbound item is administratively purged.

  The transition from Available or eligible Deferred to Claimed,
  allocation of ClaimToken, increment of Attempts, and ClaimKey mapping
  are one durable atomic operation.  An item MUST NOT be Claimed by two
  workers, selected by another ClaimKey, or purged while the claim is
  current.

  Claim expiration invalidates ClaimToken and changes the item to
  Available.  The expired ClaimKey remains mapped to that item and MUST
  NOT silently claim it again.  A consumer uses a new ClaimKey for a
  new attempt.  Renew may extend a current claim but MUST NOT revive an
  expired or resolved token.

  Claim duration is selected by trusted service policy and is not a
  fixed protocol limit.  A consumer which cannot durably finish before
  expiration renews the claim.  Processing after an unrenewed claim
  expires risks concurrent redelivery and is nonconforming.

  Consumed, Rejected, and Failed are terminal.  Consumed means the
  consumer durably accepted responsibility as defined in section 5.
  Rejected is a permanent local application refusal.  Failed is a
  permanent local service failure requiring administrative handling.


5. Consumer Outcomes
───────────────────

  A consumer acknowledges an item only after its processing result and
  every responsibility transferred from the mailer are durable.  This
  includes local delivery and duplicate records, every applicable
  onward copy, and every enabled authentication reply or notification.
  Any transfer to another durable owner MUST satisfy TSP‑0002 section
  9 for the exact retained item and all of its remaining obligations.

  Before acknowledging an Origin-Valid or SignedOrigin-Valid EchoMail
  or standalone distribution File, the consumer MUST durably commit
  every applicable TSP‑0002 Send-To copy from the exact retained item,
  receiving local identity, and immediate peer.  TITH NetMail Route is
  not applied.  The consumer obtains or recovers the complete result
  of that commitment.  A later outbound failure belongs to the new
  durable owner and does not reopen the inbound item.

  A converted or locally presented object is terminal local delivery
  and MUST NOT become the source for onward copies.  The exact signed
  payload and its validation evidence remain the distribution source;
  conversion cannot be an alternate distribution mechanism.

  Only an Origin-Valid or SignedOrigin-Valid item has a distribution
  obligation.  An Unsigned, Origin-Invalid, or SignedOrigin-Invalid
  item is final-delivery or administrative work and MUST NOT produce
  an onward copy.

  Acknowledge changes Claimed to Consumed.  The state transition and
  local result are durable before success is returned.  Once Consumed,
  the consumer and not the inbound payload export is the durable source
  of any resulting local or outbound work.

  Release changes Claimed to Available without recording an application
  failure.  Defer changes Claimed to Deferred and records a retry
  Timestamp and description.  Reject changes Claimed to Rejected and
  records a permanent local description.  All invalidate the current
  ClaimToken after atomically committing their new state.

  A result operation repeated with the exact token which performed its
  transition returns that completed outcome.  A different, expired, or
  superseded token MUST NOT alter the item.  This makes loss of a local
  IPC result recoverable without repeating consumer side effects.

  Local Rejected is not TTS‑0005 Rejected.  Transport acceptance was
  already completed.  A rejection notification, reply Message, or
  administrative report is new outbound work and uses its normal
  submission and routing policy; it does not rewrite the inbound item.

  5.1 Final Item Authentication Policy
  ──────────────────────────────────
  A final delivery consumer SHOULD have trusted local policy for each
  Message and standalone File ItemAuthentication value other than
  Origin-Valid.  For each value, the policy selects exactly one primary
  action:

    Deliver-Warn
    Orphan

  It independently enables or disables Reply-Origin.  Thus a reply may
  accompany either primary action.  Origin-Valid is delivered normally
  without an authentication warning or automatic authentication reply.
  Absent configuration uses Deliver-Warn for Unsigned and
  SignedOrigin-Valid, Orphan for both Invalid states, and disables
  Reply-Origin.

  Transport requires no such policy.  A FileRequest carries no Origin,
  SignedOrigin, or Signature, so its authenticated enclosing payload
  SignedTLV is its complete and intended authentication rather than a
  reduced one.  Its only counterparty is the immediate peer, which that
  SignedTLV already authenticates, so no Origin exists to warn a reader
  about or to reply to.  A receiver unwilling to serve a FileRequest
  refuses it with TTS‑0005 Rejected before transport acceptance; once
  it is an inbound item, the ordinary outcomes in this section apply.

  Deliver-Warn delivers the item with a prominent local diagnostic.
  Unsigned and SignedOrigin-Valid use a NOTICE.  Unsigned indicates
  that Origin did not sign the item or that an earlier legacy boundary
  removed its Signature.  SignedOrigin-Valid indicates that the
  intermediate gateway's attestation is valid but Origin did not sign
  the item.  A suitable Message diagnostic is respectively:

    NOTICE: This message was not signed by its Origin, or its
    signature was removed before delivery.

    NOTICE: This message was not signed by its Origin.  Its
    intermediate gateway signature was valid.

  SignedOrigin-Invalid and Origin-Invalid use an ERROR.  Each reports
  only the applicable verification failure defined in section 2; it
  does not diagnose how that failure arose.  Suitable diagnostics are
  respectively:

    ERROR: This message's intermediate gateway signature does not verify
    under the public key selected for that gateway.

    ERROR: This message's signature does not verify under the public key
    selected for its claimed Origin.

  A Message diagnostic is prepended to the local user presentation,
  followed by an empty line before MessageText.  It MUST NOT alter the
  immutable native payload or become the source for forwarding,
  duplicate detection, authentication, or signed‐region reconstruction.
  This distinction matters because text inserted into the body can be
  mistaken for author text, quoted in a reply, or invalidate a retained
  signature.  A File diagnostic is local metadata or an adjacent
  message; File Contents MUST NOT be prefixed or otherwise modified.

  Orphan does not deliver the item to its addressed user or area.  The
  consumer first takes durable administrative ownership of the exact
  payload, authentication value, InboundID, and reason, then
  acknowledges the item.  It may instead leave or defer the claim until
  an administrative quarantine can own it.  It MUST NOT acknowledge
  and silently discard the only recoverable copy.

  Reply-Origin creates separate signed NetMail through the ordinary
  outbound submission interface.  For a Message it is addressed to
  FromUserName at Message Origin; for a File it is addressed to the
  Sysop at File Origin.  It identifies the authentication state and
  local disposition but MUST NOT quote the original MessageText or
  disclose File Contents.  The consumer records InboundID and a stable
  idempotency identity, then obtains or recovers the complete committed
  submission result before acknowledging the inbound item.  An
  authentication reply MUST NOT itself cause another authentication
  reply.  If current routing cannot address Origin, the reply is
  omitted and that result is recorded locally.

  Automatic replies are disabled by default.  Unsigned Origin is
  unauthenticated, and SignedOrigin authenticates only the gateway's
  claim about Origin.  Replying to either can send backscatter to a
  forged address.  Replayed items, many forged Origins, or broad
  EchoMail distribution can turn replies into a flood amplifier.
  Deployments which enable Reply-Origin SHOULD make each local effect
  idempotent by InboundID, suppress repeated PayloadHash, peer, Origin,
  and authentication‐result combinations, rate‐limit by effective
  signer and Origin, and provide a local circuit breaker.

  FidoNet Policy 4.07 section 2.1.7 does not require this final local
  authentication reply.  It requires a node which agreed to route an
  in‐transit message but does not forward it to return an explanation
  to the sysop of the node where that message entered FidoNet.  A
  system which is both final consumer and an obligated legacy router
  must satisfy that separate routing duty; Reply-Origin is not a
  substitute for it.


6. Duplicate Delivery and Recovery
──────────────────────────────────

  Durable transport acceptance and consumer delivery are separate
  idempotency domains.  TTS‑0005 prevents a retransmitted signed Message
  or standalone File from creating another inbound item.  InboundID and
  ClaimKey make repeated local delivery of the retained item
  recognizable.

  Local delivery is at least once.  If a claim expires after a consumer
  committed its work but before Acknowledge became durable, the same
  InboundID may be claimed again.  A consumer MUST use InboundID as an
  idempotency identity in its durable work and recognize that replay.
  This standard does not promise exactly‐once application processing.

  FileRequest has no TTS‑0009 signed‑item identity and is a
  repeatable request.  Separate accepted FileRequest values therefore
  create separate inbound items even when their requested data is
  identical.

  After restart, the Service preserves every unexpired claim.  It
  invalidates and releases a claim only when its expiration is known to
  have passed.  If the Service cannot establish a trustworthy current
  time after recovery, it MUST NOT allow another worker to claim that
  item until it can safely determine expiration or an administrator
  resolves the claim.

  Payload export creation is not application processing.  A failed or
  interrupted export leaves the item and claim recoverable.  Recreating
  an export MUST use the exact retained payload and MUST NOT decode,
  re‐encode, convert, combine, or split it.


7. Retention and Security
─────────────────────────

  The immutable payload and inbound metadata remain private to the
  mailer and MUST NOT be writable by a consumer.  Access requires the
  binding‐supplied caller identity, configured Application association,
  and a current authorized claim.  An InboundID, ClaimKey, ClaimToken,
  pathname, or native handle value alone grants no authority.

  Payload bytes may be retired after Consumed or Rejected and its local
  result are durable.  Failed payload remains available for
  administrative handling unless an administrator explicitly retires
  it.  InboundID, Application, terminal state, ClaimKey mappings,
  metadata, and result remain queryable until administrative purge.

  An explicit administrative purge may remove an inbound record only
  when it is Consumed, Rejected, or Failed and has no current claim.
  Inbound purge durably removes its remaining payload, metadata,
  ClaimKey mappings, and local result as one administrative action.  It
  is not a consumer IPC operation and MUST NOT occur as silent ordinary
  age‐based processing.

  A TTS‑0009 signed‑item identity is independent of the local
  inbound record.  Purging InboundID MUST NOT remove that identity or
  permit remote redelivery.  Only the separate explicit administrative
  action allowed by TTS‑0005 may remove it.

  Payload pathnames, peer keys, ClaimTokens, and conversion diagnostics
  SHOULD be omitted from normal logs unless needed for local diagnosis.
  A query for an unknown or unauthorized InboundID returns NotFound so
  another Application's item is not disclosed.


8. Feedback and Discussion
───────────────────────────

  The transport can return Accepted as soon as durable responsibility
  is established without pretending that application processing is
  synchronous with a network connection.  The consumer can then work
  independently while the immutable native item remains recoverable.

  Stable InboundID and expiring exclusive claims provide the strongest
  useful boundary available without requiring the mailer and an
  arbitrary tosser to share one storage transaction.  A native consumer
  or legacy adapter supplies the final idempotency record on its side.
